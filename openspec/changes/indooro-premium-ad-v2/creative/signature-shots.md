# Three signature moments — production specification (planning only)

**Master:** 26.0 s, 30 fps, 780 frames, 16:9. Frame intervals are half-open. The three ideas are one visual sentence: complexity becomes direction; the viewer enters the map; the route finishes at the product and becomes the brand. The shopper, milk target and route share one illustrative store layout. Spatial guidance in the film is an editorial visualization, not a claim that Indooro ships AR. These are future production instructions, not completed assets.

## 1. Chaos becomes direction — frames 0–119 / 0.0–4.0 s

**Visual and narrative.** A fast but legible camera moves low through a large sculptural store. Repeated shelf faces occlude the view; two junctions expose plausible alternatives without making a cartoon maze. At 1.7–2.5 s the camera rises and opens to an architectural wide: one small adult shopper pauses at the junction. At frame 75 / 2.5 s the shopper lifts Indooro. The shelves stay put. Attention changes: peripheral bays dim about one exposure stop, shelf-edge detail and distant labels subside, near depth remains crisp, the figure and product sector gain a restrained pool of light, and the first mint position cue appears. Amber uncertainty disappears. The store becomes readable because information is selected, not because walls move.

**Camera choreography.** Start around 0.45 m above floor at a 24–28 mm equivalent field of view; advance along a predetermined collision-free spline, skim one shelf edge without clipping, make one smooth banked 70–90° turn, then crane to about 3.5 m and widen enough to locate the shopper. Keep horizon controlled and motion blur modest. The camera decelerates into the human reveal; the figure must be recognizable for at least 12 frames before the phone lift. No random handheld shake. A Remotion-only version uses four depth-separated shelf planes and a continuous virtual camera transform with the same arrival composition.

**Frame progression and sound.** 0–44: low forward passage, filtered 120 BPM pulse and two passing-aisle directional ticks. 45–74: smooth turn and crane, stereo width grows then narrows; shopper appears, no VO. 75–89: phone lift; percussion and peripheral sound subtract for roughly 0.25 s. 90–119: light/contrast hierarchy settles, first short mint position ripple and Indooro route motif enters. The transition at 2.5 s must feel like a visual answer to the chaos. Avoid a barrage of whooshes.

**Preferred implementation and dependencies.** Blender Python builds a modular shelf/floor kit from one store layout, bevels only silhouette-critical edges, sets fixed emissive accents and camera spline, renders beauty plus shopper/target/lighting or object-ID masks for selective dimming. Character uses the approved sculptural model and two poses. Remotion controls shot timing, tint/exposure masks, amber-to-mint cues, position ripple, sound and final composite. Preview a low-sample 1280×720 still at start, turn and reveal, then only this 120-frame draft range. Keep illumination changes keyed to frame 75, not simulated by moving fixtures.

**Failure points and fallback.** Fast shelves can cause aliasing, clipping, disorientation or high render cost; a tiny figure can fail the human read; selective dimming can look like a filter wipe. Validate the path against collision bounds and hold a clear wide. If 3D travel fails, build the same low-to-high camera move with layered 2.5D shelf cards, masked shopper silhouette and selective grading in Remotion. Preserve the turn, wide reveal and activation-caused hierarchy change; reduce speed before adding complexity.

## 2. Enter the map — frames 120–299 / 4.0–10.0 s

**Visual and narrative.** A large phone stays near the shopper. A readable search field contains **Milch**; one result is selected. The map appears and a mint route accelerates out of a position marker. From 6.0 s onward the camera pushes toward the screen while rotating until its optical axis aligns with the route's first segment. The phone display fills the frame, yet motion continues: flat map blocks gain height and depth as the camera crosses the glass plane; the route keeps the same path and becomes a grounded line on the store floor. The viewer feels they entered Indooro's map. No blur-covered edit, arbitrary teleport or sudden camera reset.

**Frame progression, camera and perspective.**
- 120–149 / 4.0–5.0: 50–65 mm equivalent phone hero, screen near front-facing; `Milch` and one result fit safe area. Shopper shoulder remains a soft scale cue.
- 150–179 / 5.0–6.0: single selection, map replaces result; keep destination and route start visibly distinct.
- 180–209 / 6.0–7.0: continuous dolly begins; route draws with a single acceleration then controlled cruise. Camera rolls no more than about 8° and aims along the first path tangent.
- 210–239 / 7.0–8.0: phone bezel exits frame by scale, screen fills canvas; map blocks are still legible as a plane. Route and camera share projected tangent.
- 240–269 / 8.0–9.0: camera crosses the screen plane; the same map polygons extrude into shelf volumes, floor gains parallax and route thickness/occlusion changes continuously.
- 270–299 / 9.0–10.0: camera settles into a low store perspective behind the line; shopper appears at its start. Screen-space UI has fully yielded to store space by frame 270.

Use one scripted camera path and export per-frame camera matrices plus phone-screen corner projections. Keep store floor in Blender's XY plane, Z up. A single shared layout maps walkable (x,y) coordinates to UI (u,v) through a recorded affine transform; the same polyline is sampled by normalized path distance `s∈[0,1]`. At the crossing, screen route and floor route project to within roughly 2 pixels at 720p for at least 12 overlap frames. The 2D stroke and 3D mesh should be co-located, not joined by a vague bloom. The destination stays beyond the shopper's initial field of view.

**Compositing and light.** Blender renders the phone body, store, screen plane/depth or object-ID matte, route floor mesh, shelf occlusion and the continuous camera move. Map polygons are generated from the same shelf footprints; scripted height keys raise them over frames 240–269. Keep the route mesh slightly above the floor to avoid z-fighting. Remotion composites editable React/SVG phone UI onto the exported quadrilateral, using the screen matte for bezel occlusion. As the screen fills frame, Remotion carries the vector route through a 12–18-frame overlap with the projected 3D route, then withdraws the SVG only after the spatial path is established. Animate exposure from cool UI white to controlled store edge light; do not introduce a full-frame flash. Motion blur is on camera/geometry, while the phone readability hold at frames 120–179 stays sharp.

**Sound and music.** One dry select transient at frame ~150; the recognisable Indooro route sound starts with a narrow digital two-tone rise at frame 180. Its upper partial smoothly broadens into a spatial, low-frequency motion layer as the camera crosses around frame 240; it is one evolving sound, not successive generic whooshes. A musical downbeat at 6.0 s starts the draw; the bass opens at 8.0 s as shelf depth appears. Leave VO silent through the crossing. The route sound later returns, quieter, at the destination and brand.

**Dependencies, preview and failure points.** Requires approved illustrative UI, phone shell/screen matte, one walkable store layout, path geometry, map footprint generator, Blender camera/keyframe script, camera projection export and a Remotion corner-pin/mask composite. First validate three stills (phone readable, full-screen map, low store), then render this 180-frame range at 1280×720 draft samples. Check continuity frame by frame around 210–270 and watch at normal speed muted and with temporary audio. Likely failures: unreadable UI, homography drift, a visible seam at screen crossing, mismatch between map and shelves, alpha/occlusion halos, route z-fighting or a too-expensive render. Correct geometry and timing before adding glow. **Fallback:** keep one uninterrupted virtual camera in Remotion through a layered 2.5D map: map rectangles extrude as CSS/SVG shelf cards and the same vector route gains floor perspective and occlusion masks. The screen still fills frame and the viewer still enters the map; depth is reduced, not the idea.

## 3. Destination becomes identity — frames 540–779 / 18.0–26.0 s

**Visual and narrative.** The camera catches the mint line close to floor level as it rounds the final aisle corner. It does not run into a shelf: it ends at a walkable access point opposite the exact milk carton. The route's moving head slows into a precise circular destination marker. A very thin vertical light plane connects marker to shelf position, adjacent products fall slightly in contrast, and the generic milk carton becomes unmistakable without a glowing outline. The shopper enters from the established screen direction and makes a restrained reach. At contact, marker and plane resolve; a residual route segment pulls forward into darkness. Camera follows the remaining line as store geometry drops away. The line curves into the **approved** Indooro route glyph or frames the wordmark, then `INDOORO` and `Finde deinen Weg.` settle. This completes Phone → Store → Product → Brand without a disconnected black title card.

**Camera and frame progression.** 540–584 / 18.0–19.5: 0.4–0.7 m floor-level chase with route head in the foreground, controlled 35 mm perspective and one rounded corner. 585–629 / 19.5–21.0: decelerating 3/4 destination reveal; hold enough to read carton and marker. 630–659 / 21.0–22.0: shopper reach and exact destination resolution; figure can remain a 3/4 silhouette, no hand close-up. 660–704 / 22.0–23.5: camera follows residual line as shelf silhouettes fade by depth layers, then line geometry settles into approved brand element. 705–779 / 23.5–26.0: wordmark and claim are fully static and readable for 2.5 s. The final camera is fixed. No rotating phone is needed in the closing frame.

**Route and visual behavior.** The destination circle grows from the path terminal, one 1.08× pulse at arrival then settles, never bounces. The light plane is narrow, low-opacity and brief; it points to the shelf face but makes no AR claim. The route retracts only after product contact, with a continuous visible tail that can become brand geometry. If the real approved logo has no compatible glyph, the line frames the wordmark instead of deforming the logo. This avoids inventing brand artwork.

**Sound and music.** Reduce percussion as the path rounds the corner. A controlled deceleration pitch reaches the same two-tone route motif at frame ~615; product contact at ~642 is a soft tactile transient, followed by about 0.2 s of space. The motif returns as a concise two-note brand signature during frames 660–704; music resolves without a trailer boom. Recommended final VO, `Indooro. Finde deinen Weg.`, sits after the product is seen, with the tagline completed before the static hold ends.

**Preferred implementation and dependencies.** Blender renders the low chase, shelf/carton, shopper reach pose, destination light contribution and camera/depth/object-ID passes using the shared path and scene. Remotion owns route-head timing, circle/plane overlay where cleaner than emissive 3D, product contrast grading, shelf fade, brand line/glyph, approved wordmark, VO/music/SFX and 2.5-second hold. Render only this 240-frame prototype range at 1280×720 draft quality after the separate opening/phone/transition prototypes; validate endpoint collision and screen direction before full sequence. Risks include an unconvincing reach, marker detached from product, premature logo morph, unreadable hold or a glow that resembles a generic app template. **Fallback:** use a 2.5D floor/shelf/product composition and partial shopper silhouette or hand/device crop; animate the exact same path/marker/brand line in Remotion. The found product and route-to-brand continuity remain mandatory.
